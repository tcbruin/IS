Sales call transcript — Datavance x Kramer Bouwservice B.V.
Date: 12 September 2026
Participants: Sanne de Groot (Datavance), Lisa Kramer (Operations Manager, Kramer Bouwservice B.V.)
Channel: Teams call, 34 minutes

---

Sanne: Hi Lisa, great that we could make it work today. Just so you know, I'm recording the call so I don't miss anything later, is that okay?

Lisa: Yes, sure, that's fine.

Sanne: Great. So tell me, what made you reach out to us?

Lisa: Well, for a while now we've had the feeling that we don't really have a good view of our project margins. We do renovations and small-scale new builds, usually projects of eight to twelve weeks, with about five to ten people on a job. And only once a project is completely finished and all the invoices are in do we really know what we actually made on it.

Sanne: And how do you track that during a project right now?

Lisa: Every site foreman keeps his own timesheet, in a little Excel file that he emails to me every week. I then paste those manually into an overview per project. Purchase invoices — timber, concrete, installation materials — come in separately through the accounts, and I link those up by hand as well. That takes me about three, four hours every Monday.

Sanne: And once you have that overview, what do you do with it?

Lisa: Then I only see afterwards whether a project broke even or not. Last month we had a project in Bergen op Zoom where we thought things were going fine, and then it turned out afterwards that because we'd underestimated the material costs we had barely any margin left. We only found that out after the project was already finished.

Sanne: So basically you're flying blind while the job is running.

Lisa: Exactly. And that's what's so frustrating — if I'd known that a month earlier, we could still have course-corrected. Cut back on extra hours, or had the conversation with the client about additional work.

Sanne: Roughly how many projects are running at the same time?

Lisa: Usually around six, seven at a time, spread across Noord-Brabant and a bit of Zeeland.

Sanne: And are there things you've already tried to solve this?

Lisa: We briefly looked at a module in our ERP package, but that requires all the site foremen to type their hours straight into that system, and that's just not going to happen. Those guys are up on the scaffolding, not behind a laptop. The little Excel file works for them — the problem is the merging on my end.

Sanne: Got it. And just practically — who would be looking at those numbers, if you did have them on time?

Lisa: Basically just me and my brother Erik, he's the managing director. We go through the running projects every Monday morning.

Sanne: And what difference would it make for you if every Monday morning, before that meeting starts, you simply had an up-to-date overview of the margin per project, without you having to do any cutting and pasting yourself?

Lisa: That would make a huge difference. Then we can really course-correct per project in time instead of being annoyed about it afterwards. And I'd also just get those four hours a week back for something else.

Sanne: Clear. One more thing — do you have a sense of what a "healthy" margin is for your type of projects, or does that vary quite a bit?

Lisa: It does vary per type of project, but if we drop below fifteen percent we start to get worried. Above that it's okay.

Sanne: Clear, that gives me enough to get started. I'll get to work on this and come back to you with a proposal — and to make it concrete we'll also build a sample dashboard, so you can immediately see what something like this would look like for Kramer Bouwservice.

Lisa: Sounds good, I look forward to it.

Sanne: Great, I'll send you something this week. Thanks for your time, Lisa!

Lisa: Thank you, talk soon!
