Sales call transcript — Datavance x Verkerk Versdistributie B.V.
Date: 18 September 2026
Participants: Ruben (Datavance), Nienke Aalders (Operations Manager, Verkerk Versdistributie B.V.)
Channel: on site, Barendrecht warehouse, 40 minutes

---

Ruben: Hi Nienke, thanks for letting me walk the floor with you for a bit. Shall we just carry on talking here at this table?

Nienke: Yes, sure, fine. Coffee?

Ruben: Lovely, thank you. So tell me, what made you decide to call Datavance?

Nienke: It's really about our crates. We supply fruit and vegetables to restaurants and smaller supermarkets, and that goes out in crates — blue returnable crates that have to come back to us. Every driver makes the delivery, and is supposed to get a slip signed showing how many crates he drops off and how many he takes back with him.

Ruben: And does that actually happen?

Nienke: In theory, yes. In practice we have eleven drivers, and they're all accurate to different degrees. Some fill it in neatly, others scribble something on a scrap of paper that gets lost halfway through the day. At the end of the week those slips are collected and someone at the office types them into an Excel file per customer.

Ruben: Who does that typing?

Nienke: I do, together with my colleague Sven. Between the two of us that takes about five hours a week, just the typing alone. And even then we haven't solved anything, because the balances often don't add up.

Ruben: What do you mean by "don't add up"?

Nienke: Well, at the end of every quarter we draw up the balance: how many crates are we still owed by customers. And that's consistently different from what the customer thinks they have. Last quarter, according to our Excel, we had about 1800 crates outstanding with customers, spread across ninety-three customers in total. But when we checked with our biggest customer, a supermarket chain with six branches, they said that by their own count they only had 950 of our crates.

Ruben: That's a big difference.

Nienke: A huge difference, and we have those kinds of discussions with several customers. A crate easily costs eight to ten euros each to replace, so if we're structurally "missing" thousands of crates that are sitting at customers somewhere but aren't being reported back, that adds up to tens of thousands of euros a year.

Ruben: And how are you dealing with that now?

Nienke: Honestly: we shrug our shoulders. We don't have a good argument to take to the customer, because our own data isn't reliable enough to back it up. So in the end we mostly write it off as a loss.

Ruben: If you could solve this, what would it get you?

Nienke: First of all, those five hours a week of typing, we could put those to good use elsewhere. But much more important is that we'd be able to see, per customer, per week, what the current crate balance is, instead of only finding out every quarter, after the fact, that it's wrong. Then we can step in before it gets out of hand, instead of having a "yes you did, no we didn't" argument afterwards.

Ruben: Clear. A quick question about systems — where does the rest of your data live, invoicing and so on?

Nienke: Invoicing runs through AFAS. But the crates themselves aren't recorded digitally anywhere — it really is just those slips and the Excel that Sven and I keep.

Ruben: And the drivers, do they use a scanner or an app on the road, or really just paper?

Nienke: Just paper. We've considered giving everyone a handheld scanner, but that feels like a huge step, and not everyone here is that handy with technology. It really has to stay manageable for the guys on the floor.

Ruben: I understand. And who would be looking at it if you did have an up-to-date overview?

Nienke: Me, Sven, and our managing director Kees also looks at it for the quarterly meeting with the big customers.

Ruben: Great, that's enough to get started. I'll get to work on this and come back to you with a proposal, and as always we'll build a sample dashboard alongside it so you can immediately see what the crate overview would look like for Verkerk.

Nienke: Great, I'm curious to see it.

Ruben: Thank you for your time and the tour, Nienke!

Nienke: You're welcome, see you soon.

--- end of transcript ---
