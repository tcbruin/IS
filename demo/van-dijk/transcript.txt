Ruben (Datavance): Great that we can have a chat, Marloes. So tell me, what's going on at your end when it comes to sales overview?

Marloes (Van Dijk Techniek): Yes, so we're a wholesaler in industrial parts, we have about eight field sales reps who each cover their own region. Every rep keeps his own pipeline in a little Excel sheet - open quotes, expected value, expected closing date, that kind of thing.

Ruben: Okay, and how does that all come together on your side?

Marloes: Well, that's exactly the problem. Every Friday afternoon I send everyone a reminder to update their sheet, and then I manually copy everything into an overarching Excel file. That takes me about four hours every week. And by Monday it's actually already out of date, because nobody updates it over the weekend and by Monday morning there have already been new conversations.

Ruben: Four hours a week, that's a lot. And what goes wrong if you don't manage to do it in time?

Marloes: Then I simply don't have an up-to-date picture for the monthly meeting with the management team. I get asked "where are we against target" and then I actually have to say "I don't know exactly, let me check". That's not great.

Ruben: Clear. And do you have any insight at all into where deals get stuck? So in which stage the most gets lost?

Marloes: No, not really. I only see that a deal has been lost when a rep marks it manually in his sheet, and sometimes they just forget. So the real win rate figure, I wouldn't stake my life on it.

Ruben: And in terms of systems, do you use anything besides Excel?

Marloes: We have Exact Online, but we use that purely for invoicing and accounting. There's no CRM functionality in there that we use. Some reps also just note things down in their email or on paper during a customer visit.

Ruben: If you could dream, what would you want to have ready every Monday without you having to do any manual work for it?

Marloes: Just an overview - how much is open per stage, what's the expected revenue, how are we doing against target, and ideally also per rep and per region so I can see who might need a bit of a push.

Ruben: And that monthly meeting with management, how important is it exactly?

Marloes: Very important. That's the moment when we adjust course. If I'm sitting there with outdated figures, we're basically flying blind.

Ruben: Clear story. Just for scoping purposes - if we now had to choose between, say, setting up a full CRM project, or first starting purely with making visible what's already there - what would be most valuable for you to do as a first step?

Marloes: I really think the second one. A whole CRM project, my team isn't ready for that yet, it feels like a big step. I first just want insight into what we already have, without the reps having to change their way of working right away.

Ruben: Clear. Then I'll take that as the scope: a dashboard that brings the existing sheets together into an up-to-date pipeline overview, without building a whole CRM right now.
